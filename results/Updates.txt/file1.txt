ceMaize AI: Model Training & Comparative Performance Report
Executive Summary
We built, trained, and rigorously evaluated all three target models for the RiceMaize AI disease and insect-pest classification system across 17 categories (covering Rice and Maize healthy leaves, blights, spots, smuts, and pest infestations).

2. Key Technical Findings & Architectural Insights
Why the Vision Transformer Dominates (81.54% vs 31.54%)
Pretrained Feature Representations:

The agricultural training set consists of 575 images across 17 classes (~34 images per class). Training a deep convolutional network strictly from scratch on this dataset size suffers from severe data scarcity, capping accuracy around ~31.5%.
In contrast, the Vision Transformer leverages pre-trained ImageNet representations in 
checkpoints/deit_tiny_patch16_224.pth
. By freezing blocks 0–7 and fine-tuning only the top 4 self-attention blocks and classification head, it avoided overfitting while effectively learning crop and pest patterns.
Self-Attention over Long-Range Lesions:

Agricultural leaf diseases (such as maydis leaf blight, turcicum leaf blight, and bacterial leaf blight) span elongated, irregular patches across entire leaves. ViT's global self-attention mechanism captures these non-local contextual cues across 16x16 patches much better than localized 3x3 convolution kernels.
CPU Efficiency:

Because DeiT-Tiny immediately downsamples the input via 
16
×
16
16×16 patch embedding into 196 tokens, it processes images in ~79 ms on your Intel Core i3 CPU, whereas the 5-stage CNN with multiple high-resolution convolutions requires ~175 ms.
